## Executive Summary — Cribl: The AI Platform for Telemetry

For more than two decades, IT and Security innovation has been driven by
applications. Organizations adopted observability platforms to monitor systems,
SIEMs to investigate threats, analytics tools to generate insights, and countless
specialized solutions to solve emerging operational challenges.

Each of these applications delivered value. Each also introduced its own telemetry
infrastructure, data stores, schemas, integrations, and operational requirements.
As organizations adopted more solutions, they accumulated more copies of the same
data, more infrastructure to operate, and more complexity to manage.

For years, this tradeoff was acceptable because applications were the primary
source of value.

The AI era changes that equation.

AI agents, copilots, and automated workflows do not care which application
generated the data. They care about access to the data itself. As organizations
deploy AI across IT and Security, telemetry becomes the critical raw material that
powers people, applications, and agents alike.

This shifts the center of gravity from individual applications to the platform
that makes telemetry accessible, usable, and actionable.

Every IT and Security experience of the next decade will be built on a telemetry
platform.

Security, observability, analytics, automation, AI visibility, AI SOC, agentic
operations, and future experiences we have not yet imagined will all depend on
access to the same underlying telemetry.

Organizations are not starting from a blank slate. Years of technology investments
have left telemetry scattered across observability platforms, security tools,
cloud services, data lakes, and operational systems. The challenge is no longer
collecting data. The challenge is accessing, governing, and operationalizing it
across an increasingly complex environment.

The first challenge is not centralization.

The first challenge is access.

Before organizations can unlock the value of their telemetry, they must be able to
find it, understand it, govern it, and use it wherever it resides.

Cribl did not begin with a vision of building another SIEM, observability
platform, or analytics solution. We started with a simpler problem: helping
organizations control and unlock the value of their telemetry.

Over time, solving that problem required us to build collection, routing,
transformation, governance, storage, federation, search, and access capabilities.

What emerged was something larger than a telemetry pipeline or a collection of
products.

We realized the industry had spent twenty years building solutions. In the
process, it had accidentally rebuilt the same telemetry infrastructure over and
over again.

Every new category brought another pipeline, another data store, another schema,
and another copy of the same telemetry.

We are not building another solution.

We are providing the shared telemetry infrastructure that every solution needs.

That realization led to the AI Platform for Telemetry.

Today, Cribl connects people, applications, and agents to IT and Security data
wherever it resides. Powered by the Data Engine for IT and Security, Cribl enables
organizations to collect, manage, govern, access, and act on telemetry at AI
scale.

Some experiences will be built by Cribl. Others will be built by partners,
customers, and AI itself. All of them will depend on the same telemetry platform.

The market is just now realizing it needs a telemetry platform.

Cribl already has one.

## Technical Narrative — Why IT and Security Need a Telemetry Platform

Most IT and Security architectures evolved one solution at a time.

Organizations purchased a SIEM to solve security analytics. They adopted
observability platforms to monitor applications. They added threat detection,
automation, asset intelligence, AI visibility, and dozens of other specialized
tools as new requirements emerged.

Each solution solved a legitimate problem. The challenge is that most solutions
were designed as independent products, each with its own telemetry infrastructure.

As a result, organizations often collect the same telemetry multiple times, store
it multiple times, govern it multiple times, and pay for it multiple times.
Security and observability teams frequently depend on much of the same underlying
telemetry while maintaining separate pipelines, separate storage systems, separate
schemas, and separate operational processes.

This pattern exists across the industry.

Many vendors market broad solution portfolios, but those portfolios are often
composed of products built by different teams, acquired at different times, and
architected independently. While the commercial experience may appear unified, the
underlying telemetry infrastructure frequently remains fragmented. Different
products maintain different storage layers, different schemas, different search
experiences, and different operational requirements.

Observability platforms face similar challenges. Logs, metrics, traces, AI
telemetry, and emerging workloads are often managed by separate systems with
separate scaling characteristics and operational overhead.

The result is an architecture where telemetry infrastructure is repeatedly rebuilt
by every solution.

Historically, organizations tolerated this duplication because applications
delivered enough value to justify the cost. Today, however, telemetry volumes
continue to grow, budgets remain constrained, and AI introduces entirely new
demands on the underlying data.

AI visibility platforms, AI SOC solutions, agentic operations, and future
AI-native experiences all require access to telemetry. Most vendors respond by
asking customers to send them another copy of the data.

This approach does not scale.

The challenge is no longer collecting telemetry. Most organizations already
collect more telemetry than they can effectively use. The challenge is providing
secure, governed access to telemetry across people, applications, and agents
without continuously duplicating infrastructure.

This is the architectural pattern that is breaking under the weight of AI.

Every new solution requires another telemetry stack. Every new category introduces
another set of integrations, storage systems, governance controls, and operational
overhead.

The industry spent twenty years building solutions. In the process, it
accidentally rebuilt the same telemetry infrastructure over and over again.

We are not building another solution.

We are providing the shared telemetry infrastructure that every solution needs.

A telemetry platform addresses this problem by providing the common capabilities
nearly every IT and Security solution requires: collection, routing,
transformation, enrichment, governance, storage, federation, search, and access.

Instead of rebuilding these capabilities within every solution, they are provided
once and shared across many experiences.

This changes the economics of IT and Security architectures. Organizations can
collect telemetry once and use it across multiple solutions. Data can remain where
it makes the most sense. Existing investments continue to provide value. New
technologies can be adopted without rebuilding telemetry infrastructure from
scratch.

Most importantly, it allows organizations to separate telemetry infrastructure
from the applications that consume it.

Security, observability, analytics, automation, AI visibility, AI SOC, and future
AI-native experiences can all operate from the same telemetry platform rather than
maintaining independent telemetry stacks.

The goal is not to replace every application.

The goal is to eliminate the need for every application to build and operate its
own telemetry infrastructure.

Every IT and Security experience of the next decade will be built on a telemetry
platform.

The organizations that establish that platform today will be best positioned to
adopt the technologies of tomorrow.
